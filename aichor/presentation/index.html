Refinement: a small win here, a uniform loss there

On ProteoBench, gated refinement adds +0.0018 to +0.0039 pep/mass. On the internal held-out sets the same operation on the v1.3 checkpoint is negative in 17 of 17 datasets, and 34 of 34 dataset-arm pairs across both checkpoints. That conclusion does not transfer — it reverses.

Three modes now, and the split is consistent: pep/mass within ±0.004 (+0.0005 greedy, +0.0017 beam-10, −0.0037 greedy+ref) while pep/exact gains +0.0338 to +0.0455. Mass matching forgives isobaric swaps and exact does not, so a gain that appears only at exact level points at modification and I/L calls — what a 133-residue vocabulary buys — with backbone sequencing unchanged. It held when tested at beam width, including in a mode whose pep/mass went the other way.

Internal held-out sets, identical hardware throughout.

  • The two generations cost the same per spectrum — agreeing within 2% at both greedy and beam-5. Cost measured on one arm transfers to the other.
  • Refinement costs more than the decode it refines, and its price barely depends on what it refines (8 h 42 m on greedy, 8 h 57 m on knapsack beam-5). Refined modes are not cheap add-ons.
  • Knapsack costs 7.2× plain beam at the same width — 121 h against 16 h 50 m. On ProteoBench the same ratio was 2.5×, so this penalty did not transfer.
  • Diffusion-only is 2.3× slower on v1.3 — the one mode where the generations diverge.

The confidence scores do not mean what they say

ProteoBench nine-species balanced · InstaNovo v1.2.2 · beam-10 · Winnow calibrate-estimate

tolerance0.005 (exceeded ~9×)

accepted PSMs504,692
true error rate there9.40% (nominal 5%)

Winnow calibrate-estimate on beam-10, Koina running in-pod as a Triton sidecar. Reproduced to 16 significant figures on a rerun.

At the 5% FDR operating point the true error rate is 9.4% — roughly 47,000 wrong peptides among 504,692 accepted, where the FDR promises about 25,000.

The reliability curve sits below the diagonal everywhere, so this is systematic rather than localised, and the gap widens as the score falls: near 0.99 it nearly touches the diagonal, at the 0.781 threshold empirical correctness is only ~0.75.

The miscalibration is worst exactly where the FDR cut is made.

Least trustworthy on the hardest organisms

ProteoBench nine-species balanced · InstaNovo v1.2.2 · beam-10 top-1 · FDR-accepted set

The aggregate −0.044 averages over a three-fold range. The two worst-calibrated species are also the two with the highest unsolved rates, so the confidence is least reliable exactly where a user would most want to lean on it.

One number hides a sign change

ProteoBench nine-species balanced · InstaNovo v1.2.2 · beam-10 · all 721,739 scored PSMs

Across the whole score range over-confidence is only +0.0121 — because the ends cancel. The model is under-confident at the bottom and over-confident in the middle, with the top decile nearly perfect. This is why the tail-focused sTECE at the operating threshold is the more useful summary for FDR control: it looks only where the threshold actually falls.

Measured on all 721,739 scored PSMs using ProteoBench's own precision-coverage definition. Spearman between the two scores is 0.9275, so the calibrated score genuinely reorders PSMs — the reordering is just mildly harmful.

A Winnow-rescored submission would not move ProteoBench's AUC. What calibration buys is a confidence that means what it says, which FDR control requires and a raw log-probability cannot offer. That is a real result, and not a leaderboard improvement.

Two scorers: different by construction, agreeing in practice

Internal held-out, 17 sets · greedy decoding · v1.2.2 · same predictions, scored twice

propertyProteoBench DenovoScoresInstaNovo Metrics

Same parameter names, same defaults-shaped API, different physics. On a ~100 Da residue ProteoBench allows 0.002 Da against InstaNovo's 0.1 Da; on a 1000 Da prefix, 0.05 Da against 0.5 Da. Identical in v1.2.2 and v1.3.0 — this is a scorer difference, not a version one.

Despite tolerances differing by 10–50×, the per-dataset means differ by under 0.003, and ProteoBench is marginally looser in 14 of 17 — the opposite of what tolerance alone predicts, because its bidirectional prefix/suffix alignment offsets the tighter threshold. Both scorers agree the v1.3 checkpoint is behind: −0.82 pp by InstaNovo's, −1.02 pp by ProteoBench's.

So the conflicting chart is not a metric problem. Its v1.3 figure (0.5772) matches our v1.3 run under InstaNovo Metrics exactly; its v1.2.2 figure (0.5745) matches neither of ours (0.5854, 0.5883). The two sides are different experiments — the v1.2.2 baseline is another run. An earlier version of this deck blamed the scorer; that was wrong.

Where this stands

Settled

  • Beam search is the intervention that pays. Knapsack is not, and this is now measured on both benchmarks: +0.0005 on ProteoBench, +0.0010 internally for 7.2× the GPU.
  • v1.3.0's gain over v1.2.2 on ProteoBench is confined to exact matching (+0.034 to +0.046 across three modes, pep/mass within ±0.004) — modification and I/L calling, not backbone sequencing.
  • Gated refinement is marginal on ProteoBench and negative on our data.
  • The confidence is over-confident by ~4.4 points at the operating threshold, worst on the hardest organisms, and recalibration does not improve ranking.
  • Cost transfers between checkpoints; accuracy conclusions do not.

Open

  • Four v1.3.0 modes on ProteoBench still running: diffusion-only and both knapsack modes, plus beam-10+refinement. The two refined ones are queued for re-scoring, since they run on an image predating the N-terminal length fix.
  • Which split is canonical. Our matrix runs on ninespecies_v1; a re-split exists and an earlier v1.2.2 evaluation used it. If the re-split is the intended test set, both arms are on superseded data and would need re-running. Where it physically lives is unconfirmed — ninespecies_v2 is referenced by one script and used by no run.
  • Five internal runs scored but not yet in the pooled table — both arms' beam-10 and beam-5+refinement, and v1.3 diffusion-only. Three v1.2.2 internal modes remain unlaunched.
  • Why diffusion-only is 2.3× slower on the v1.3 checkpoint.
  • A calibrator trained on a species-held-out split, to separate domain shift from intrinsic miscalibration — and to cover the five modes that keep no beams.
